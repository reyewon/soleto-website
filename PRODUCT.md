# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Southampton diners and visitors choosing where to eat, usually on a phone, often within a day or two of the meal. Three main contexts:

- **Pre and post-theatre guests** at the Mayflower Theatre directly across the road, deciding on dinner around a fixed curtain time. This audience skews older.
- **Couples and families** booking a special occasion or a good neighbourhood meal, wanting reassurance that it will feel a cut above without being stiff.
- **Private-dining and catering enquirers** (birthdays and corporate dinners in the 14-seat room; weddings and events of 30+ off-site across Hampshire).

The job to be done is nearly always the same: decide Soleto is the right choice, then book a table with as little friction as possible.

## Product Purpose

A marketing site for Soleto, a family-run Italian trattoria opposite the Mayflower Theatre in Southampton, run by chef George Jiojioras (Greek roots, Italian craft) and Dominika Jiojioras (front of house), open since 2017.

Its job is to turn a curious visitor into a reservation, while establishing Soleto as a distinct, classy, family-run restaurant that is unmistakably its own place, and being found in both Google and AI search (it competes directly with neighbours for "Italian near the Mayflower"). Success is bookings through the Dojo widget, and being the obvious choice for a meal near the theatre.

## Brand Personality

Classy, Sicilian, family. Fine dining that still feels like a family table, southern-Italian warmth with real craft behind it, never fussy or corporate.

Voice: warm, plain-spoken, first person ("we opened Soleto in 2017", "you will probably meet one of us at the door"). Concrete and specific over salesy; the food and the family do the selling. Greek soul, Italian craft, one family.

## Anti-references

The site was rebuilt specifically to shed the generic AI-generated look, so the anti-references are sharp and non-negotiable:

- **Max's Brasserie**, the Italian restaurant next door at No. 9, whose site is textbook vibe-coded: cream canvas, high-contrast editorial serif with an italic accent, mono/tracked-caps labels, "big serif headline left, small body right" grids, dark-overlay photos with a quote on top splitting sections, numbered chapter markers. Soleto must never be mistakable for it.
- The AI-default type stack: **Fraunces + Inter Tight**, and 0.27em tracked-caps eyebrows above every section.
- Vibe-code copy tells: **full stops on short headers**, tricolon/fragment-triad cadence ("chosen that morning, pulled by hand, on the board by the time you sit down"), and the "in the heart of Southampton" cliché (which collides with the neighbour's own headline).
- Generic Squarespace-Italian-restaurant templates and the warm-cream-plus-serif AI monoculture in general.

## Design Principles

- **Don't look like next door.** Visible differentiation from Max's (and from the AI-template look generally) is a first-class requirement, not a nice-to-have. Every type, layout and copy choice should be identifiably Soleto's own.
- **The room, on the screen.** The site should feel like the actual dining room: painterly, old-world, gilt-framed, candlelit. That feeling is carried by real photography, an all-serif printed-menu-card typography, film grain, and restrained ornament, not by loud effects.
- **Family voice, not marketing voice.** Copy speaks plainly in the first person, specific and human. No slogans, no tricolons, no AI cadence, no full stops on short headers.
- **Every path leads to a table.** Reserve is always within reach on every page; the booking is the point, but it persuades through atmosphere and specifics rather than hard-selling.
- **Quiet craft over loud effect.** Class comes from restraint and detail: signature moves (ticket panels, oxblood seams, drop caps) are used sparingly, at most once per page, so they stay special.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target **WCAG 2.1 AA**: body text at least 4.5:1 against its background, full keyboard navigation, visible focus, and `prefers-reduced-motion` honoured on every animation (Lenis smooth-scroll and reveals included).

Extra care for the older theatre-going audience: generous tap targets and comfortably large, high-contrast type; do not chase "elegant" light-grey body text. The gold brand colour is decorative only (rules, keylines, ornaments) and must never carry text, as it fails contrast; this is already encoded in `globals.css` (`--gold` is documented as non-text).
